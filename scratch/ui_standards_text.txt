Page | 14    UI/UX Design Principles and Best Practices 1. Clarity and Simplicity  User-Centered Design: Begin with user research—identify your users’ needs, pain points, and behavior patterns. Design ﬂows and interfaces around real user goals, not just business logic.   Minimalistic Design: Remove unnecessary elements. Prioritize content and actions that users care about most. Use whitespace to guide focus and reduce visual noise.   Readability: Use legible fonts with proper sizing and line height. Stick to a maximum of 2–3 font families. Ensure text color contrasts well with its background.   Line Length: Keep line lengths between 50–75 characters per line for body text. This prevents eye strain and improves reading speed.   Text Hierarchy: Use a clear typographic scale: o H1 for page titles o H2 for section headings o H3 or bold text for subpoints o Standard body text for content This helps users scan and understand content faster.

Page | 15    2. Consistency  Visual Consistency:  Use a predeﬁned design system or theme. Apply the same color palette, icon style, typography, and button appearance throughout the app.    Behavioural Consistency:  Ensure interactions behave the same way everywhere. Actions, such as button clicks or gestures, should behave in a consistent way across the application.

Page | 17   4. Accessibility  Keyboard Navigation: All interactive elements (links, buttons, form ﬁelds) must be reachable via the Tab key and activated with Enter or Space.   Alt Text for Images: Provide meaningful alt attributes for all informative images. Decorative images should have alt="" to be ignored by screen readers.   Tab Indexing: Use the tabindex attribute responsibly to deﬁne the focus order, ensuring logical and intuitive navigation, especially in custom components or modals.    

--- Page 18 ---
Page | 18   5. Interaction Users should always understand what is happening in response to their actions. Lack of feedback leads to confusion or repeated inputs.  Instant Feedback: Show visual cues like button states (pressed, loading), or display messages after form submissions or actions.   Error Handling: Use inline error messages near the ﬁeld with the issue. Be speciﬁc and actionable: “Email is required” is better than “Invalid input.”   Loading Indicators: Display spinners, progress bars, or skeleton loaders when data is being fetched or processes are in progress.      

--- Page 19 ---
Page | 19   6. Responsive Design Users access apps on a variety of devices. Your interface should adapt seamlessly across screen sizes and orientation.  Flexible Layouts: Use CSS Grid or Flexbox to create layouts that adjust based on screen width.    Mobile-First Design: Design for smaller screens ﬁrst, then scale up. Ensure content stacks vertically and inputs/buttons remain touch-friendly.   Orientation Handling: The interface should work correctly when switching between portrait and landscape on phones and tablets.

Page | 20   7. Performance and EƯiciency  Fast Load Times: To achieve Fast load Times, try to compress images, lazy-load content, and minimize third-party scripts. Use browser caching and CDN services.   Smooth Transitions: Use subtle animations for modal openings, tab transitions, and dropdowns to enhance interactivity—but avoid overly complex or distracting eƯects.
